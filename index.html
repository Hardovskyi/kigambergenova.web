03 Frames of Practice

Frames of Practice

The record shows where I worked. These frames show what those experiences changed in the way I observe, contribute, and build.

01 Japan — Observe 02 Texas — Contribute 03 India — Build

01 — Japan · Observe

I learned to see before I learned to respond.

Context

Study abroad across six cities. Daily sketch practice while studying SANAA, Kengo Kuma, Sou Fujimoto, and Tadao Ando in person rather than through photographs.

Response

I documented how light enters a room, how a threshold shifts what the body expects, and how material restraint can say more than expression — sequence, atmosphere, and precedent before intervention.

Developed

Spatial observation, threshold awareness, material reading, and attention before proposal.

Means for a Team

I begin by understanding context, use, and atmosphere before imposing a design response.

02 — Texas · Contribute

Architecture begins with listening.

Context

A Habitat for Humanity week in Houston — demolition, patching, painting, and landscaping inside homes that would soon belong to families. No drawings; a crew, a site, and people who needed help.

Response

I learned the crew’s workflow, took practical tasks without needing design authorship, and stayed attentive to what the house would hold for the people who would live there.

Developed

Service, teamwork, adaptability, and respect for lived environments.

Means for a Team

I can enter an unfamiliar workflow, understand what is needed, and contribute without requiring ownership of every decision.

03 — India · Build

Concept to a built community room in eight weeks.

View the built project ↗

Context

Design-build moved design out of the studio into coordination, labor, weather, and construction sequence for a community room in Maharashtra.

Response

When a mid-project site relocation forced a redesign, I replanned foundations and the structural grid in five days — keeping design intent clear through drawings, assembly diagrams, and construction support.

Developed

Constructability, site coordination, technical communication, and protecting intent when conditions change.

Means for a Team

I understand that design must remain clear, buildable, and adaptable when real conditions challenge the original plan.

04 What I Bring to Practice

Why this path makes me useful on a team.

Five hiring-relevant habits — drawn from the work, the record, and the frames above.

  1. I observe before proposing.

    I read context, behavior, constraints, and patterns before defining a response.

  2. I communicate ideas clearly.

    Teaching, publications, diagrams, presentations, and visual work trained me to translate complex ideas for different audiences.

  3. I adapt without losing intent.

    Design-build taught me to revise solutions when conditions change while protecting the project’s core purpose.

  4. I connect concept to execution.

    I move between research, spatial strategy, documentation, detailing, and making.

  5. I contribute to teams.

    I listen, coordinate, support others, and take responsibility where the work needs it.
